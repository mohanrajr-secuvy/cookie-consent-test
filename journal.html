211 miles, one tent: testing the Ridgeline on the John Muir Trail

Lena Okafor, Senior product designer
October 14, 2026 · 9 min read

The Ridgeline started as a sketch on a napkin at Tuolumne Meadows. Two years and fourteen prototypes later, we wanted to know whether it could survive the trip that inspired it: the full John Muir Trail, Yosemite Valley to the summit of Mount Whitney, in a single push.

Four of us carried production-ready samples. Our only rule was that nothing got babied. Tents went up in the dark, came down wet, and got stuffed into the bottom of packs that were already too full.

Days 1–4: Granite and thunderstorms

Afternoon storms rolled in like clockwork above Donohue Pass. The first real test came at Thousand Island Lake, where gusts flattened two of the three other tents at our campsite. The Ridgeline's crossing-pole hub held, but the leeward guy-out pulled through its webbing loop. We had a fix sketched before dinner.

“A tent you can't repair in the field is a tent you'll eventually abandon in the field.”

Days 5–9: The long middle

Evolution Basin is where gear either earns its place or gets mailed home from Muir Trail Ranch. Our boots were on their third river crossing of the morning. The Trailblazer's resoleable midsole is heavier than a foam runner, but nobody's feet blistered, and the toe caps took the granite without delaminating.

Days 10–12: Up and over

Forester Pass at 13,200 feet. Whitney at dawn. By the time we walked into Whitney Portal, the Ridgeline had been pitched 11 times in rain, hail, and one memorably calm night under a full moon. We changed three things before launch: reinforced guy-out points, a longer vestibule zip, and a stuff sack you can actually get the tent back into.
